Compress image to 100 KB: free, private and in your browser

Need a picture under 100 KB for an email, a website or an online form? Set the limit once and iLoveImages finds the highest quality that stays under it automatically, for every size you have switched on.

If an image is still too heavy at low quality, the tool can reduce its dimensions step by step until it fits, and tells you when it did.

How to compress an image to 100 KB

  1. Load your image.
  2. Leave Compression on "Target size" with 100 KB (or type another limit).
  3. Choose JPEG or WebP for the smallest files.
  4. Wait for the status light to turn green; each card shows the final size and quality used.
  5. Download the file that matches your requirement.

Frequently asked questions

How does the tool hit an exact file size?

It encodes the image repeatedly, binary-searching the quality setting until the file is as large as possible without going over your limit. The result lands just under the target, never over it.

Which format gives the smallest file?

WebP is usually 25–35% smaller than JPEG at the same visual quality. Use JPEG when the destination does not accept WebP.

Is iLoveImages free?

Yes. Every feature is free with no sign-up, no watermark and no limit on the number of images.

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Cropping, editing, resizing and compression all happen on your device using your browser. Your photos never leave your computer or phone, so the tool is private and works even on slow connections once the page has loaded.

Which image formats are supported?

You can open JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF, BMP, AVIF and HEIC (where your browser supports it). You can export to JPEG, WebP and PNG, and to AVIF in browsers that can encode it.

How do I make an image smaller in KB?

Switch Compression to "Target size" and enter the maximum file size in KB. The tool finds the highest quality that fits under your limit for every size, and can shrink the dimensions if quality alone is not enough.

Can I resize one image to many sizes at once?

Yes. Turn on any built-in viewports (desktop, tablet, mobile, Instagram, YouTube and more) or add your own width and height, then download each file or all of them as a ZIP.

Will resizing reduce image quality?

Downscaling uses multi-step, high-quality resampling to keep edges sharp, and the "Never upscale" option stops small images from being stretched and blurred. You can inspect every output at 100% before downloading.
